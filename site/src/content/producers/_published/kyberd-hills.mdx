Kyberd Hills is a Mornington Peninsula producer with no published street address; the harvested record ties it only to the region. The category here is recorded as estate winery, based on the personal ownership narrative on the site, though the page does not state whether the fruit is grown on the property or bought in from other growers.

The winery makes Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Tempranillo, according to the producer's own account. Michael Kyberd is named as winemaker and owner, and the site describes him as having spent nearly three decades making wine on the Mornington Peninsula. Before Kyberd Hills, he is said to have been chief winemaker for a prominent local brand, and to have subsequently worked as a contract winemaker.

Nicolette, whose earlier surname was Hills, is credited as co-founder. The site sets out how the two met: Michael Kyberd met her in London, at the residence of Dan Hills' sister, and she relocated to Australia within six months, after which they married. Dan Hills is named separately as a mentor. The producer places this meeting as "over 15 years ago" but does not state when the winemaking business itself was established, so no founding year is given here.

The producer's site states that private tastings are available for special events, which points to a by-appointment cellar door rather than a public one. No hours, fees or address are published for it. Nothing in the record indicates an online shop, so wines are not listed here as available for purchase online or shipped nationally.

No organic or biodynamic certification is claimed for the vineyard, and no specific farming practice, such as wild fermentation, unfined or unfiltered bottling, or minimal sulphur use, is stated for these wines. Nothing in the record points to vessel type or to a published production volume, so those fields are left unknown rather than assumed.

Two further details from the site are worth recording as the producer's own claims rather than as this directory's assessment. The name Kyberd is said, according to the site, to mean "dagger" in Danish. The producer also states that the wines are made to mature and build complexity over a decade or more, a claim about longevity rather than a tasting note, and one repeated here without having tasted the wine.
