Lobethal Road sits between the Adelaide Hills villages of Lobethal and Mount Torrens, in the region's north-east corner, roughly 40 kilometres from central Adelaide. The record gives no street address beyond this, and no suburb, which the producer's own site appears to leave equally imprecise.

The site names Dave Neyle and Inga Lidums as the owners. No corporate parent, shared address, or shared contact domain turns up in the record, which is what supports treating the operation as independent. No founding year is stated, and no grape varieties are named on the page, so neither appears here.

The cellar door is open Thursday to Monday, 11am to 5pm, with wine tastings described as available every day. The producer's page also carries a note that the site is closed on Thursday 23 July for a private function, reopening Friday 24 July on the usual 11am to 5pm hours. That kind of one-off notice suggests the calendar on the producer's own site is worth checking directly rather than relying on a standing weekly pattern.

Food service runs alongside tastings rather than as an afterthought. A seasonal lunch menu is offered Friday, Saturday, Sunday and public holidays, and on those same days a Feed Me menu is available at $80 per person, with the option opened up to groups of eight or more. A local cheese platter is offered on the two days the seasonal lunch menu is not running, Monday and Thursday. A wood fire is mentioned as available for lunch, which points to some form of cooked or warmed food service rather than a cold-plate arrangement, though the record does not go further than naming the fire.

The setting notes describe a garden that overlooks the vineyard, offered as a place to sit during warmer months. Beyond that description, no further detail on plantings, block size, or winemaking approach appears in the record, and none is supplied here as a result. The determinations section returns no result for organic or biodynamic status, no stated fruit source, and none of wild ferment, unfined, unfiltered, or minimal sulphur practices, so all four practice flags are recorded as false rather than left to guess.

Because the record does not state a founding year, list varieties, or describe winemaking method, this entry stops short of the fuller picture a longer harvest might allow. Bookings for tastings and lunch are described as highly recommended rather than mandatory, which is why the cellar door is classified here as open rather than by appointment. Anyone planning a visit around the Feed Me menu or a specific date should treat the producer's own site as the current word, given the record's own example of an unscheduled closure.
