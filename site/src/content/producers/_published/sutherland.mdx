Sutherland's website does not name a suburb, state or region, and none of the ownership, address or contact information usually used to place a producer is present on the site. The vineyard is described in some detail, but where it sits is not stated, so no location or region is recorded here.

The estate is built around three acres of Shiraz on an east-facing block, planted with two clones the producer distinguishes by origin: an old Tahbilk clone and an old Barossa clone. The producer describes the Tahbilk clone as fruit-driven with vibrant colour, and the Barossa clone as the source of structure and depth. A steeper, south-facing section of the same vineyard carries Grenache, Mourvèdre and Cinsault, planted close together and trained as bush vines.

Before planting, the site was deep ripped and given a soil nutrition programme, according to the producer. The vineyard went in over two stages, in 2012 and 2014, and viticulturist Tim Brown provided guidance through those foundation years. The producer states that pruning and shoot thinning are managed closely to keep yields low, which is consistent with the close planting and bush vine training described for the Grenache, Mourvèdre and Cinsault block.

In the winery, the producer states that grapes are handpicked to select only the best quality fruit, and that pressing is done with a basket press, described as a traditional technique. The wine is matured in a combination of French and American oak barrels for twelve to eighteen months, then racked and bottled once ageing is complete. No further detail on fermentation vessels, filtration or sulphur handling is given, so nothing beyond what is stated is recorded here.

No founding year is stated for the business itself. The two dates on record, 2012 and 2014, mark stages of vineyard establishment rather than the date the winery began operating, and the two should not be read as the same thing. The site provides no ownership statements, no ABN and no parent company mentions, and independence is marked as unresolved pending that information. There is no cellar door, tasting or pricing information published, and no online sales channel is stated, so none of that is recorded here either. What the record supports is a small estate vineyard, planted in two stages on ground prepared specifically for it, farmed for low yields, and made into wine using traditional pressing and extended oak ageing.
