Tapanappa is a South Australian producer working three named vineyard sites: Foggy Hill, on the Fleurieu Peninsula; the Tiers vineyard; and the Whalebone Vineyard. The record gives no street address or suburb, so what follows is limited to the state and to the vineyards the producer names for itself.

Foggy Hill is described as sitting on ground underlain by a 550-million-year-old geological formation, from which the winery takes its name. The producer states that "Tapanappa" is derived from a local Aboriginal language, and that it implies "sticking to the path."

</Pull>

The producer's own copy frames the current operation as a continuation of Brian Croser's long winemaking career, rather than as a new venture. Croser is named in the record as the pioneering winemaker behind the project, and the site describes the wines as the continuation of a 40-year mission.

<Pull attribution="Tapanappa, on their site">
  continuation of the 40-year mission of pioneering winemaker Brian Croser and his family
</Pull>

The same copy states that a next generation of family is closely involved, without naming who they are or setting out who currently owns or controls the business. That leaves the ownership structure unconfirmed from what the site publishes: family involvement is claimed, but no family name or ownership stake is given, so "family-owned" is not a description this entry can support. What can be said is that the producer presents the business as a family continuation of Croser's work.

Fruit for the wines comes from the three named vineyards, and the record treats this as estate-grown rather than purchased. The varieties named are chardonnay, pinot noir, cabernet franc and merlot, and three specific wines are named as examples: a 2019 Tiers Chardonnay, a 2018 Foggy Hill Pinot Noir, and a 2016 Whalebone Vineyard Cabernet Franc Merlot blend. No organic or biodynamic certification is claimed for any of the plantings, and none of the four winemaking practices tracked here, wild ferment, unfined, unfiltered, or minimal sulphur, are stated in the record.

There is no published tasting room address, no cellar door hours, and no setting description in the record, so no visit can be confirmed or described. The one route to purchase named in the record is a wine club: members receive three or six-bottle packs of Tapanappa wines, including museum releases, four times a year, with a discount of up to 20 percent on orders. No separate online shop page or general pricing list appears in the record beyond that club structure.

The site itself requires visitor age verification for entry, which is a general legal gate on the website rather than a stated condition for any physical visit, and the record contains nothing else to describe hours, bookings, or on-site facilities.
