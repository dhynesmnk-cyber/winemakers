Dandelion Vineyards is based in McLaren Vale, South Australia. The producer's own description of its winemaking is that fruit from what it calls its heirloom vineyards is blended using artisan winemaking traditions.

<Pull attribution="Dandelion Vineyards, on its winemaking page">
  Blending of fruit from heirloom vineyards with artisan winemaking traditions
</Pull>

No founding year appears on the site. The page refers to "decades of experience" and to heirloom vineyards without giving a date range or planting year for either, so no founding date or vine age can be recorded here.

Two wines are named on the site: the Love Letter from McLaren Vale Fiano and the Field of Wonders Chardonnay, matching the fiano and chardonnay varieties the producer lists among its own determinations. A Lemon Myrtle Spritzer also appears among the site's wine names, though its composition is not described, so it is not counted among the varieties or styles recorded for this entry.

Elena Brooks is named on the site as winemaker and founder. Whether the business is family owned is not stated outright. The founder's name and the description of heirloom vineyards suggest a family connection, but the page makes no explicit statement of ownership, so that description is not used here.

Tastings take place in a space the winery calls the Wonder Room, open seven days a week from 11am to 5pm. The site also lists a Winemaker's Long Table Lunch with Elena Brooks and a "Vintage 2025 experience", the latter marked as sold out at the time the page was recorded. A Wine Club membership is offered, though the site gives no pricing for the club or for any of the wines, so no cost figure is recorded.

The site describes the operation as a "Five Red Star Winery". No issuing body, year, or criteria is given for that rating, so it is reported here only as a claim the producer makes about itself, not as a verified award.

Fruit for the wines is described as coming from the producer's own heirloom vineyards, with no mention anywhere on the page of fruit bought from other growers. That is why the fruit source is recorded as estate rather than mixed or purchased. No winemaking practices such as wild ferment, unfiltered or unfined bottlings, or minimal sulphur additions are mentioned on the site, and none are recorded here as a result. No vessel types, such as oak or concrete, are named either.

No street address is published, only the suburb and state, and no annual production figure appears anywhere on the site, so production is recorded as unknown rather than estimated.
