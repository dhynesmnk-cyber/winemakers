Barristers Block runs a cellar door in the Adelaide Hills, described on its site as being about 30 minutes from Adelaide. The record gives no street address, only the general locality. Fruit for the wines is drawn from the Adelaide Hills and Wrattonbully, according to the regions the producer lists, though the record does not break down which wines come from which region or state how much, if any, fruit is estate-grown.

The name is the clearest piece of history on offer. The producer states that the vineyard was the subject of a six-year legal battle during harsh farming conditions in the 1990s, fought to save the vineyard from being lost, and that the winery took its name, Barristers Block, from that fight. No further detail is given: no names of the people involved, no account of who the dispute was with, and no founding year for the winery itself. The only other date on the page is architectural rather than corporate: a Hayshed on the property, built in the 1850s, has been converted for use as a dining space and now hosts a winter event called "Fireside Feed Me", a long lunch described as taking place in a relaxed setting.

The cellar door itself is open seven days a week, from 11am to 5pm, with no booking required for a general visit. Food is part of the offer: a tasting and grazing platter, lunch, and an afternoon stay are all mentioned, and casual dining can be booked for groups of up to 10 in an undercover, self-service space. The property is also described as home to deer and geese, a detail worth noting for anyone visiting with children or dogs, though the record gives no further context on how the animals are kept or whether they are part of a formal attraction.

Barristers Block describes itself as a family winery, though the record names no family members and gives no founding generation, so that description is reported here as the producer's own claim rather than as an established fact of ownership. The site also mentions "Boston Bay" as a label it operates, but offers no detail on the relationship between the two names: whether Boston Bay is a separate brand under the same ownership, a joint venture, or something else again is not stated, and so is left unresolved.

On production, the record is thin. No varieties are named, no winemaking methods are described, and no certifications, organic or otherwise, are claimed. A "Winter Reds Best Sellers 6-Pack" is mentioned among the producer's offerings, which indicates that red wine is part of the range, but nothing further can be drawn from that about which grapes are used or how the wines are made. No pricing is published for the cellar door tastings or the food service, and there is no indication of online sales or national shipping on the pages the record draws from.
