Berrigan Wines operates from Nairne, in the Adelaide Hills region of South Australia, drawing its fruit from an estate vineyard on site. The record also names Mount Benson among the producer's regions, though nothing in the source explains how that second region connects to the winery's fruit supply, and no separate vineyard is described there.

The producer's site states that the business is family owned and operated by Dan Berrigan and Wendi Ticao. That is the extent of the ownership detail the record carries: no founding year, and no account of how the two names relate to one another beyond the shared statement.

Five varieties appear in the record: Grüner Veltliner, Chardonnay, Sangiovese, Shiraz and Cabernet Sauvignon. Current listings include a 2023 Grüner Veltliner, a 2024 Chardonnay, a 2025 Sangiovese, and a 2024 "Shining Rock" Shiraz, with an earlier 2022 "Shining Rock" Shiraz and a 2021 Cabernet Sauvignon both marked sold out. Prices for the available wines run from $24.95 to $34.95 AUD, but these figures sit in the varieties list rather than a dedicated pricing section, so no cost line is carried into this entry.

The site describes its wines as small batches made in the winery, and states that all wines are vegan-friendly, though no certifying body is named for that claim. On farming, the producer states no organic or biodynamic certification and no such practice; the confidence notes on the source page mention a general reference to being sustainable in the vines, which stops short of a certified or even a stated practice, so the entry records both organic and biodynamic status as none.

No cellar door hours, tasting fees, or appointment details are published on the site, so this entry carries no cellar door arrangement. The record likewise gives no founding year, no production volume, and no online shop link, so those fields are left unset here rather than filled in from guesswork.

The producer's own material also describes a Bull Rider logo, said to represent the courage to take a chance and have a crack, and to symbolise strength and resilience. Beyond that, the record offers no history section and no narrative of how the vineyard at Nairne came to be planted, so none is supplied here.
