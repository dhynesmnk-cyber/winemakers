Basket Range Wine is at Basket Range, in the Adelaide Hills. Phillip Broderick bought the property in 1978, when it was planted to apple and cherry orchards, and put the first grapes in the ground in 1980. The first vintage followed in 1983 and ran to 100 dozen cases. The original plantings were a mix of Bordeaux varieties, and the producer states that the cabernet sauvignon, merlot and petit verdot were among the first of those varieties planted in the Adelaide Hills.

In 2000 the family bought a larger neighbouring property, which had also been orchard, apples and pears, and replanted it with 4.5 hectares of vines. The holding is 13.5 acres sitting at about 450 metres of elevation, and it is not a single flat block: there are three hillside aspects, two facing north and one facing south-east. The varieties are matched to them. Pinot noir and chardonnay are on the cooler, more sheltered south and east-facing slopes; cabernet sauvignon, merlot and petit verdot are on the north and west-facing slopes. The producer describes the underlying geology as an ancient bedrock of sandstone, with ironstone and quartz distributed through blue and red clay.

For much of that time the winery was a small part of the operation. Most fruit went to other local wineries, and Phillip made only small quantities under the Basket Range Wine label. That arrangement ended in 2016, when Sholto Broderick took over winemaking. Sholto studied viticulture and oenology and completed vintages in Europe and locally, and he and his brother Louis grew up in the vineyard helping with picking and pruning. Since 2016 every grape grown on the property goes into the Basket Range Wine label, and the wines are made at an on-site winery.

The stated approach in the winery is a simple one, using less manipulation, with the goal of making wines that reflect the different sites, aspects and varieties across an undulating vineyard. In the vineyard, the producer describes a focus on organic practice, vine rejuvenation and longevity, farming to preserve soil microbiology and to allow a diversity of plants and insects. No certifying body is named anywhere on the source page, so this entry records the vineyard as practising organic rather than certified. That is the ordinary position for a grower of this size: certification costs money and takes years of continuous compliance, and 4.5 hectares does not always carry the expense. The distinction matters to a reader deciding what a label claim is worth, which is why it is spelled out here rather than smoothed over.

No cellar door hours, tasting arrangements, tasting fees or prices appear on the source page. Nothing is recorded here about visiting, and anyone wanting to buy or taste should contact the winery directly through its website. Two generations, one hill, and forty-odd years of continuous family ownership from the 1978 purchase to the present is what the record supports.
