Oliver's Taranga sits in the Seaview sub-region of McLaren Vale, on land the Oliver family has held since 1839, when William Oliver bought an unseen parcel and sailed with his wife Elizabeth and two children from the Scottish border country to South Australia aboard the ship Delhi.

The estate runs across the 100 hectare Taranga property in McLaren Vale, and all of the fruit is grown there rather than bought in. Shiraz, Grenache and Mataro remain the core plantings, alongside varieties the producer says it was first to bring into South Australia: Sagrantino and some of the earliest Fiano plantings in the state. Tempranillo and Vermentino are also grown. A block planted in 1948 with help from the local Kaurna people still stands on the property.

Six generations have worked the land since William Oliver's day. His son Archibald inherited part of the holding; Archibald's son RW Oliver diversified into currants, wool and cattle alongside the vines; RW's son HJ took the business over in 1970 and turned it into a grape-only operation. Corrina Wright, granddaughter of HJ's sister Robyn, made the first wine to carry the Oliver's Taranga name in 1994, the family's first move from grape growing into winemaking under its own label. Corrina now runs the business alongside her cousin Brioni Oliver, and Brioni's nephew Sam Oliver joined the operation in 2019. The producer states that ownership has stayed within the family: both properties are still proudly in family hands.

A heritage-listed workers' cottage on the property was converted into a cellar door in 2005 by Brioni and Corrina, and redeveloped with a modern extension in 2021 after Sam Oliver helped secure a state government grant for the work. No opening hours or booking requirements are given on the source page.

No organic or biodynamic certification is claimed on the source page, and no detail is given on winemaking practices such as wild fermentation, fining or filtration. The wines named in the record span red table wine built on Shiraz, Grenache and Mataro, white wine from the estate's Fiano and Vermentino, a fortified Grenache called The Banished, and a sparkling wine, The Hunt for Mrs Oliver, named for HJ's wife Marjorie.
