Mt Bera Vineyards sits on the northern aspect of the Adelaide Hills, west of Gumeracha, on land that traces back to an 1851 commercial orchard. The estate covers 157 hectares, all of it estate-grown, and the property is described on the producer's about page as certified organic and biodynamically grown. No certifying body is named for either claim, so the certification is recorded here as stated, and the biodynamic practice as practising rather than certified.

The Horner family moved from Sydney to South Australia in the 1990s, buying the property with what the site describes as a temporary move that became a lifelong commitment. The family frames the operation as a family affair, and the about page builds its history around that framing:

<Pull attribution="Mt Bera Vineyards, on their about page">
At the heart of Mt Bera Vineyards is a story of family, learning, and community.
</Pull>

The property was affected by the 2015 Sampson Flat Bushfire. The producer states that the recovery which followed brought the vineyards back to full production. The Horners continue to run the operation with their children and a growing team, according to the site.

Plantings run wider than most Adelaide Hills estates. Alongside the familiar pinot noir, cabernet sauvignon, shiraz, merlot and pinot gris are blocks of blaufrankisch, gruner veltliner, tempranillo, sangiovese and touriga nacional, along with zweigelt, mencia and malvazija, none of which appear on the region's usual variety list. The producer describes some of this fruit as going into limited-production, single vineyard and micro terroir lots, a description of its own bottlings rather than a claim this entry can verify against a named block.

The about page calls the wines "estate-grown, natural wines," but does not state whether ferments are wild, whether wines go unfined or unfiltered, or whether sulphur additions are minimal. None of those specific practices are confirmed, and none are recorded here as a result.

There is no cellar door information published on the about page: no hours, no tasting fee, no mention of bookings or visitation arrangements. There is likewise no stated online shop. A reader wanting to visit or buy directly would need to check the site's other pages, since the about page this record is drawn from covers history and ownership only.
