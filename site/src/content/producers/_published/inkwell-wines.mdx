Inkwell Wines is an estate winery at 377 California Road, Tatachilla, in South Australia's McLaren Vale region. The producer farms twelve hectares of vineyard on site and describes the vineyard as Regenerative Organic Certified®. The record does not give the name of the certifying body behind that mark, only the mark itself, so the certification is best read as the producer's stated status rather than a verified registry entry.

The producer states that fermentation has used indigenous yeasts since 2005, and frames this as part of a wider approach to minimal intervention, with no additions or corrections made to the wine.

<Pull attribution="Inkwell Wines, on their winemaking page">
  One tractor, one de-stemmer, one press, small fermenters without artificial cooling.
</Pull>

The producer also sets out a longer-term aim for the operation:

<Pull attribution="Inkwell Wines, on their history page">
  Twelve years vision to be Australia's most sustainable super premium estate winery and net negative carbon producer.
</Pull>

Two ranges are produced from the property. Inkwell is made solely from estate fruit grown on the Tatachilla vineyard. Dub Style draws on grapes from other vineyards and varieties as well as the home block. Both ranges are described by the producer as vegan friendly, and both are sold online and from the tasting rooms.

The tasting room is open Tuesday to Sunday, from 12 pm to 5 pm. The property also includes Hotel California Road, a micro hotel with three suites. The record describes each suite as having a private deck overlooking the vineyard, a soaking tub, a king-size bed, a twin rain shower, a leather couch, and a mini-bar stocked with Inkwell wines, along with original Aboriginal artwork and Persian rugs.

No ownership statement, ABN, or parent-company mention appears in the record. Independence could not be positively confirmed on that basis, though no evidence of external ownership was found either. The record's mention of an ABN and shared-address data returning nothing is itself the reportable fact here, rather than a gap to fill with assumption.

The record gives no founding year for the business itself: 2005 marks the start of the indigenous-yeast fermentation practice, not the establishment of the winery, and the two are kept distinct here because the source keeps them distinct.
