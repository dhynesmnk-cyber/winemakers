Moorooduc Estate sits at 501 Derril Road in Moorooduc, on the Mornington Peninsula south-east of Melbourne. Richard and Jill McIntyre bought the property in 1982 as an empty paddock on a hill, and it has been the site of the winery and cellar door ever since. Richard McIntyre was, by the record's account, a surgeon before he took up winemaking here. The first vines went into the ground in 1983: red Bordeaux varieties, chardonnay, and two rows of pinot noir. First production followed in 1986, one barrel of cabernet sauvignon, and the first commercial pinot noir release came in 1990.

The estate's fruit today comes from five vineyards on the peninsula. The original McIntyre Vineyard, planted in 1983 and expanded through further plantings of pinot noir, shiraz and chardonnay between 1994 and 2006, remains the base. The producer also leases and manages the Garden Vineyard, part of the Robinson and Osborn Vineyards within five kilometres, and the Dunns Creek Vineyard to the south-west. That mix of owned and leased and managed fruit is the reason the record marks fruit source as mixed rather than estate. Plantings have shifted over time: the original Cabernet Block was removed in 2020 and replanted to pinot noir in 2024, and ten nebbiolo vines put in among the Shiraz Block in 2017 were followed in 2023 by a further half acre of shiraz pulled out for nebbiolo. In 2023 the property marked forty years of vines at the McIntyre site.

Winemaking is handled by a team of three: Richard McIntyre, Jeremy Magyar, who joined in 2016 as winemaker, viticulturist and general manager, and Kate McIntyre. The record describes small batch wild yeast ferments, hand plunging, foot-trodden whole bunch ferments and grapes and skins moved by shovel. Wild yeast trials began in 1996, following a trip to Burgundy the year before.

On farming, the producer states that soils are farmed regeneratively and that this shift intensified between 2020 and 2025, with a consultative role held by Dr Dylan Grigg over that period. No certifying body is named for either organic or biodynamic status, and both are recorded as none rather than practising or certified. That is the ordinary position for a vineyard describing regenerative work without a formal scheme behind it: the practice is stated, the paperwork is not.

The winery building was designed by Gregory Burgess and built between 1999 and 2001, bringing capacity to 200 tonnes. Jill and Richard moved to Moorooduc full-time in 1999. Between 2000 and 2010 the site also ran Jill's Restaurant and a bed and breakfast, managed by Jill and David Chapman. A Single Vineyard range was introduced in 2010, the same year the Estate range broadened to draw on more than the McIntyre vineyard alone. Through the 2020 to 2022 lockdowns the producer began online tastings under the name "Drinks With Kate," which the record states continue today. The site also acknowledges that the property sits on the land of the Bunurong/Boon Wurrung people.
