Serrat is a small estate winery about three kilometres from Yarra Glen township, in the Yarra Valley. The first hectare of vines went into the ground in 2001, planted to pinot noir, chardonnay, viognier and grenache, on a north-facing site of grey to grey-brown clays with buckshot gravels through the topsoil and a subsoil of red-brown clay impregnated with ancient, deteriorated mudstones. The producer states the soil is low in fertility. Vines are planted at a density of 8,800 per hectare, and the producer notes that, on average, one vine produces one bottle.

Further plantings followed over the next sixteen years: 1.6 acres of shiraz in 2003, more Mendoza chardonnay completed in 2007, an acre of nebbiolo, barbera, malbec and grenache noir in 2010, the first grenache blanc vines recorded in Victoria in 2016, and a further acre of nebbiolo and shiraz in 2017. All fruit is drawn from these estate blocks.

Tom Carson graduated as a winemaker in 1991 and worked vintages in Burgundy before joining James Halliday's Coldstream Hills. He went on to become chief winemaker at Yering Station, where his team was named Winemaker of the Year at the 2004 Wine and Spirit International Competition in London. He ducked the Len Evans Tutorial in 2002 and was, with the 2012 Yabby Lake Block 1, the first winemaker awarded a Jimmy Watson trophy for a pinot noir. Since July 2008 he has also held the roles of general manager and chief winemaker for Kirby Family Vineyards, which owns Yabby Lake on the Mornington Peninsula and Heathcote Estate; this outside role is disclosed on the Serrat site and is recorded as not affecting the independence of the Serrat operation. He is also recorded as a judge on the national and international show circuit and as a member of the Qantas wine panel for twelve years.

Nadège Carson was raised to take over her family's vineyard in the Banyuls appellation of southern France, and came to Australia for a vintage in 1990. She was marketing manager for Yarrabank from 1993, then for Yering Station from 1996 until 2004, before working as strategic project manager for Rathbone Wine Group until 2008. In 1992, she and Tom set up Mercurey Australia, a business importing French barrels. Kate Thurgood and a team member named Julie, whose surname is not given in the record, are also named as part of the operation.

No organic or biodynamic certification is mentioned for the property, and the record does not state whether any farming practice such as wild fermentation, fining, filtration or minimal sulphur use applies here; all four are recorded as not stated.
