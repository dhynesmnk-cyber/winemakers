Paringa Estate sits on a ten-acre property in Red Hill, in the Mornington Peninsula, on land that was originally a north-facing orchard. Lindsay McCall, then a geography teacher, bought the property in 1984 and cleared it to plant vines the following year. The record credits the decision to a 1980 Seville Estate shiraz that McCall tasted in a restaurant in 1983. Local vignerons at the time advised against planting shiraz in the peninsula's cool maritime climate; McCall planted it anyway, in the warmest spot on the property.

The home vineyard sits on gently undulating slopes at Red Hill, and the producer's site describes the vines there as being in their third decade. Single Vineyard parcels on Paringa Road supply the top tier of the range. All fruit is estate-grown, drawn from the three varieties Paringa Estate plants: shiraz, pinot noir and chardonnay.

The producer organises its wines into three series: Peninsula, Estate and Single Vineyard. Estate wines are drawn from the mature home vineyard and Single Vineyard wines from the Paringa Road parcels. The producer distinguishes the three by fruit source rather than by any stated difference in method. No fermentation, fining or filtration practices are stated on the record, and no organic or biodynamic certification is claimed. No pricing for wines or tastings appears in the source material.

The cellar door is open every day from 11am to 5pm, with bookings recommended rather than required. Staff offer guidance on tasting, and the cellar door windows look down into the working winery. The property was named Best Cellar Door at the Gourmet Traveller WINE awards in 2017, and Best Large Cellar Door in 2020; a judge from those awards, Jenni Port, is credited on the site with contributing the cellar door's description.

A restaurant operates alongside the cellar door. Lunch runs Wednesday to Sunday, and dinner is served on Saturday evening, for which bookings are described as essential. Garden dining runs Wednesday to Sunday from midday to 3pm on a walk-in basis, at Cyprus trestle tables seating twelve under umbrellas, though groups of eight or more can book ahead. The dining room is described as overlooking the home vineyard, with views into the kitchen. The garden menu is described as changing regularly; examples given include Abrolhos scallops with XO sauce, Sher wagyu paillard with anchovy and lemon, heirloom tomato with stonefruit and chevre, and cured snapper with herb and buttermilk dressing. In winter, the garden menu is replaced with cheese, charcuterie and oysters. Flinders Island milk-fed lamb is served through the restaurant.

The record attributes one further view to the winemaker: that the estate's pinot noir is becoming more mature and complex as the vines age, a claim made by McCall rather than independently assessed here.
