Ngeringa is an estate winery at an address the producer gives simply as Mount Barker Summit, within the Adelaide Hills wine region. Janet and Erinn Klein have owned the property for more than two decades, and the winery is family-owned, its owning family named directly in the material collected.

The first vines were planted on the site in 2001, with the first vintage following in 2005. The property is farmed under biodynamic principles: the producer describes biodynamic winemaking practices but does not name a certifying body, so the status recorded here is practising rather than certified. No organic certification or claim is made. All fruit is estate-grown; the record lists no purchased fruit and no named grape varieties, so neither variety nor wine style can be reported.

The vineyard sits within a wider biodynamic operation. Native grasses grow between the rows, and native revegetation extends across the property beyond the vines themselves. No detail on fermentation vessels, filtration or sulphur use is given in the material reviewed, and the record's practice fields are all recorded as not stated.

Cellar door service runs Saturday and Sunday, 11am to 4pm. Guided tastings are offered, wine is available by the glass, and light snacks made with ingredients from the property's biodynamic farm are served alongside them. A separate Vine-to-Glass tour and tasting experience runs on Fridays and Sundays, a more structured circuit through vineyard and produce garden ahead of the tasting itself.

Beyond the vineyard, the property carries a seasonal biodynamic market vegetable garden, olive and fruit orchards, and free-range livestock, extending the biodynamic approach into a working farm rather than a vineyard alone. A subscription programme, the Solstice Club, is described as an exclusive wine club with twice-yearly deliveries, though no pricing or minimum commitment for it is stated.

No tasting fee, cost information or online shop is published in the material collected, so none of these appear here. Production volume is not stated, and no award or specific vineyard name is given, so none of those claims can be made either.
