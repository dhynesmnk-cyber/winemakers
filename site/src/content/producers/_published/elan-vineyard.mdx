Elan Vineyard sits on a north-easterly slope at Balnarring, on the Mornington Peninsula, at 110 metres above sea level. The site is protected by tree lines but still reached by cooling southerly breezes. The block covers 2.5 hectares and was planted in the early 1980s; no founding year for the winery itself is stated.

The vineyard is not irrigated and is described as low yielding, with viticultural practices said to expose fruit to dappled sunlight. Winemaking is described as estate grown and made in small quantities, with wild yeast used in fermentation. The producer states that this approach is intended to produce wines that reflect the vineyard site.

That statement is the extent of what the record gives on ownership: no family name, no founding figure, and no ABN or shared address to verify it against. The independence marker on this record is set to "check" for that reason.

Seven varieties are grown across the block: Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Gamay, Blanc de Blanc, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. The Blanc de Blanc is made using the traditional method and aged on lees. French barriques are named as a vessel for at least some of the reds and the Chardonnay.

The cellar door is open every weekend from 11am to 5pm, or by appointment, with an additional public holiday opening over the Queen's Birthday long weekend described in the record as a "Winter Wine Weekend." The property offers BBQ and picnic facilities, a rotunda overlooking the vineyard and a playground.
