Main Ridge Estate sits on Red Hill, in the Mornington Peninsula region of Victoria. The record places it in a bush valley, with the winery describing sweeping views across its vines to the surrounding bushland. It was established in 1975, making 2025 its fiftieth year, and the site is described as a small family winery.

The named fruit is chardonnay, grown across what the producer calls three single-vineyard wines, each from a different vineyard within the Main Ridge hinterland. The Harvester's notes flag that "single-vineyard" and "estate" language here comes from the producer's own description rather than a confirmed account of every parcel, but the record supports naming chardonnay as the variety and treating the fruit source as estate-grown on that basis.

Ownership is not fully spelled out on the source page. The record names James, Libby and Tim Sexton alongside founder Nat White, who established the property in 1975 and died on 20 June this year, after a long illness with Parkinson's disease. The page does not state how the Sexton family and White's founding role relate to each other in terms of current ownership, so that connection is left as the record leaves it: named individuals, without an explicit statement of who owns the business today.

Cellar door visits run Friday to Sunday, with tastings held in the winery's historic building. Bookings are capped at eight people, which points to a bookings-required arrangement rather than casual walk-in access. The producer states that relaxed lunches, snacks and drinks are offered, and that visitors can settle by the fire in winter or out on the deck on fine days. Pre-booked, leashed dogs are welcome.

No pricing, tasting fee, production volume, or online sales channel appears in the record, so none of those fields are stated here. Likewise, the record does not name a region formally beyond "Main Ridge hinterland," a descriptor rather than a Geographical Indication, though Red Hill itself is a recognised subregion of the Mornington Peninsula and is used as such in this entry. No organic or biodynamic certification is claimed, and none of the four winemaking practice flags (wild ferment, unfined, unfiltered, minimal SO2) are indicated in the source material.

What the record supports, in sum, is a small chardonnay-focused winery on Red Hill, fifty years old in 2025, open for limited weekend visits by booking, with a founder's history that the current page treats with some care and a current family connection that it does not fully explain.
