Sir Paz Estate sits in the southern ranges of the Yarra Valley, Victoria, on what the producer describes as gently sloping hills of red volcanic earth. No street address is published; the location is given only by region and geographic description. The vineyard has been producing wine since 2001, and the producer states that every wine it makes comes exclusively from grapes grown on that property, with none brought in from elsewhere.

<Pull attribution="Sir Paz Estate, on their vineyard page">

</Pull>

The estate lists eight wines under production: Shiraz, Merlot, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Rosé, Blanc de Blanc, Sparkling Shiraz and Pinot Noir. Recent plantings of Pinot Noir are noted, with a first vintage still to be released. Vines are hand-pruned and fruit hand-picked across the property, according to the producer.

Winemaking is described as gentle extraction with minimal intervention. The producer states there is little to no fining or filtration, which this record treats as an explicit unfiltered practice rather than an inference from silence. Wines are matured in French oak, though the record does not specify barrel format.

The cellar door is open Friday, Saturday and Sunday from 10:30am to 5pm, with Thursday visits available by appointment only. A guided tasting flight is offered, and the producer also lists rare back-vintage wines available for tasting and purchase alongside current releases. Groups of five or more are directed to arrange a visit by email rather than through a standard booking, which suggests smaller groups can attend without prior arrangement during published hours.

A woodfire restaurant operates on the same three days, from midday to 5pm. It runs an à la carte menu the producer describes as designed to match the estate's own wines, built around a wood-fired oven and barbecue within the dining area. The producer's own description of the setting emphasises views: both the cellar door and the restaurant are said to look out over the vineyard and the wider Yarra Valley, with diners also able to watch the woodfired cooking in progress.

No pricing is published in the record, so no cost figure or tasting fee appears here. No production volume is stated either, so the entry records the case count as unknown rather than estimating one. The record contains no ownership signals pointing to a parent company, no shared address or contact domain, and no ABN; independence is recorded as clear on the strength of the producer's own statement rather than a registry search.
