Elysian Springs sits on Eden Valley Road at Mt Pleasant, South Australia, on a 160 acre estate the producer places at the birthplace of the River Torrens. The property is described as the most northern vineyard in the Adelaide Hills wine region, and a natural spring on site is stated to supply the vineyard's water.

The vineyard was established by the Seppelt family in 1984, according to the producer's own history page. That page makes no statement about who owns or operates the business today, so current ownership cannot be reported here. The original planting, also from 1984, comprised Shiraz, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Pinot Meunier, five varieties spanning both red and white wine. Whether that planting has since been extended or replaced is not stated, so the varieties listed reflect what went into the ground in 1984 rather than a confirmed current inventory.

Fruit for the wines is drawn entirely from the estate. The producer's winemaking notes describe "minimalistic viticultural practices" and characterise the operation as biodynamic, organic and following other alternative methods, without naming a certifying body for either organic or biodynamic status. That absence matters: a vineyard can be farmed to organic or biodynamic standards for years without paying for the audit and paperwork that certification requires, and the language on the page reads as a description of method rather than a claim of accreditation. It is recorded here as practising rather than certified, in line with what the producer states and does not state.

No winemaking practices beyond the general biodynamic and organic description are detailed. Wild fermentation, unfined or unfiltered bottling, and minimal sulphur use are not mentioned on the page, so none of those practices are attributed to the wines.

The site notes a cellar door and vineyard at the Eden Valley Road address, which places a physical visiting point on the property, though no opening hours, booking requirement or tasting fee are published. Pricing is likewise absent from the page. Anyone wanting current details on visiting, tastings or cost would need to contact the producer directly, since the source material this entry is drawn from does not carry them.

The regional placement is worth noting on its own terms. The address sits on Eden Valley Road, in the corridor where the Eden Valley and Adelaide Hills wine regions meet, but the producer's own material assigns the vineyard to Adelaide Hills and describes it as that region's most northern site. That framing is taken at face value here, since it is the producer's stated position and no contradicting registry information is available.

Because so much of the standard profile of a producer, current ownership, hours, pricing, certification, is silent on this page, this entry stays close to what is confirmed: an estate-grown vineyard near the top of the Adelaide Hills region, planted by the Seppelt family in 1984, farmed by organic and biodynamic method without a named certifier, and offering a cellar door whose terms are not published.
