Staindl Wines is based at Red Hill, in the Mornington Peninsula region of Victoria. The estate vineyard sits at Red Hill South, on a site the producer describes as containing red-iron buckshot gravel. All fruit used in the wines is grown on this property, with plantings of Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. Because the vineyard is named and the fruit source is stated as estate-grown throughout, this is a single-vineyard operation in the strict sense the record supports.

The producer states that vineyard management follows organic and biodynamic principles. No certifying body is named for either practice, so both are recorded here as practising rather than certified, which is the ordinary position for a small estate: certification is a formal process with its own costs, and a producer can farm to organic or biodynamic standards for years before, or instead of, seeking the paperwork. The winemaking notes go further than most producer statements of this kind by naming the specific requirement behind biodynamic certification.

<Pull attribution="Staindl Wines, on their winemaking page">
Biodynamic certification requires strict compliance with organic and biodynamic practices on every living part of the property.
</Pull>

The same notes describe vine management in terms of choosing the kindest and most sustainable available solution, though no specific technique, such as wild fermentation or minimal intervention in the cellar, is stated in the record, and none of those practices are claimed here as a result.

Four wines are currently listed for sale: a 2025 Riesling at $35.00, a 2022 Gewürztraminer at $52.00, a 2024 Chardonnay at $52.00, and a 2019 Pinot Noir at $62.00. The Gewürztraminer is marked as limited stock, with the producer asking buyers to order one or two bottles at a time rather than in larger quantities. Riesling, Gewürztraminer and Chardonnay are white wines; the Pinot Noir is a red. Wines appear to be sold directly through the producer's own website, which lists prices for each current release.

The producer's logo is described as a stylised letter S, said to represent the red-iron buckshot gravel of the Red Hill South vineyard. Colours are assigned to each variety in the range: deep red for Pinot Noir, golden yellow for Chardonnay and a green accent for Riesling. This is the kind of detail that belongs in a record because it comes from the producer's own account of itself, not because it tells a reader anything about how the wine tastes.

The record contains no published cellar door hours, no stated tasting fee, and no setting or history details, so none of those are given here. No founding year and no named people appear in the source material either, and nothing in this entry should be read as filling that silence with a guess.
