Simon Tolley Wines works from Simon Tolley Lodge in Woodside, in the Adelaide Hills. The one variety the record names is pinot noir, and it states that this fruit is grown entirely on the estate, with nothing purchased in from other growers. No founding year is given, and no winemaking detail beyond the fruit source is in the record.

The producer's own account of itself centres on lineage rather than history:

<Pull attribution="Simon Tolley Wines, on their history page">
  Simon Tolley is a fifth-generation Vigneron
</Pull>

The record does not name the four generations that came before, and gives no founding date for the property or the label, so there is nothing to corroborate the claim beyond the statement itself. For the same reason, independence is flagged as an open question rather than confirmed: no parent company or shared address is disclosed, but the ownership signal on offer is a lineage claim without the names or dates that would let it stand as documented fact.

The cellar door hosts tastings and, according to the record, events, described by the producer as informative and engaging. The same material calls the cellar door award-winning, though it does not name a specific award, so that description is reported here as the producer's own characterisation rather than a claim the directory can stand behind. Two animals are named as part of the site: Gytrash, a thoroughbred rehomed at the cellar door in 2022 through the Life After Racing program, and Tilly, a kelpie the producer credits with assisting in vineyard and sheep management oversight.

On the commercial side, the only figure in the record is a shipping threshold: orders over $150.00 ship free. There is no stated price for the wine itself, no production volume, and no mention of a minimum age policy or booking requirement for the cellar door, so none of those are given here.

No certification is claimed for organic or biodynamic practice, and the record does not describe any of wild fermentation, unfined or unfiltered bottling, or minimal sulphur use, so those are recorded as not stated rather than assumed absent. The variety list is narrow, a single red grape, which is consistent with an estate-only fruit source and a small planted area, though the record gives no figure for either hectares under vine or annual cases produced.
