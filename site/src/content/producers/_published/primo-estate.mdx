Primo Estate is based in McLaren Vale, South Australia, on a vineyard planted in 1973 by founder Primo Grilli. Primo arrived in South Australia in 1953, at eighteen, from the Le Marche region of Italy, and settled in Virginia, a small township north of Adelaide. He met his wife Santina in Adelaide; the pair had grown up less than a kilometre apart in Italy. The vineyard, planted twenty years after his arrival, was followed by a winery building in 1979.

Joe Grilli, the eldest of Primo and Santina's children, took over winemaking and graduated Dux of the Roseworthy winemaking course. Under his direction the estate developed a Double Pruning technique for Cabernet and uses botrytis on its Riesling fruit. Joe was among the first Australian winemakers to work with Italian grape varieties, and the producer states that two wines, The Tuscan and The Venetian, are made in Italy each year using traditional Italian grapes and a modern Australian winemaking approach. The winery has also trialled Amarone and Ripasso methods.

The current headquarters, with its cellar door and tasting room, opened in McLaren Vale in 2006. The producer describes the space as combining modern design with Italian practicality and reflecting the family's multicultural background. No opening hours or tasting fee are published in the record.

The next generation of the family is now involved in the business. Daniel Grilli, Joe's eldest son, has also graduated Dux of the Roseworthy winemaking course, making the pair, according to the producer, the only father and son to have both achieved this distinction in Australia. Matteo Grilli, Joe's second son, is training as General Manager, and Elena Grilli, Joe's daughter, works in the cellar door on weekends.

The producer's own account frames the winery this way, and the record supports it: the family is named across three generations, from Primo and Santina through Joe to Daniel, Matteo and Elena.

The named varieties are Cabernet, Riesling, Colombard and Italian varieties, with Colombard described on the producer's page as its most popular wine. No organic or biodynamic certification is claimed, and the record does not state whether fruit is estate-grown or purchased. Primo Estate also produces olive oil.
