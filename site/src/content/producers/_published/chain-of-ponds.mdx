Chain of Ponds is an estate winery on the outskirts of Gumeracha, in the Adelaide Hills, roughly 4km from the original township of the same name. The vineyard was established in 1985, with an initial planting of four thousand vines across nine varieties, 470 vines of each. A further 150 acres went into the ground in 1989, and the producer's own history page counts this among the first plantings of any note in the northern part of the Adelaide Hills wine region. The first vintage followed in 1988.

The name comes from a stretch of water rather than a person. The Chain of Ponds originally described a creek running from Kersbrook to its junction with the River Torrens near Prairie, in a district recorded as having ponds that held water through the driest summers. Most of that chain of water holes disappeared under the Millbrook Reservoir in 1918. The settlement itself was first called Philptown, after Oliver Philp, the founding publican of the Morningstar Hotel, before the township took the name Chain of Ponds in 1864. It served as a postal village and one of four changing stations on the Adelaide to Mannum coach route from the mid-1850s, in a neighbourhood noted for its vineyards and fruit trees. Wine grapes had been planted in the Adelaide Hills as early as the 1840s, and an 1865 notice records the sale of a winemaking plant on the same "Chain of Ponds" section, belonging to a Swithen Farmer, with several thousand gallons of wine, presses, fermenting vats, casks and 16 acres of vineyard. That operation predates the current business by well over a century and is a separate enterprise, not a forerunner of it.

Fruit for the present-day wines comes from a mix of sources. The 1985 and 1989 plantings were estate vineyard, and the record states that the winery has since extended its vineyard boundaries to source fruit from a broader area of the region, which it says allows for greater varietal distinction and complexity. No specific vineyards, varieties or vintages are named in support of that broader sourcing, so the fruit source here is recorded as mixed rather than estate.

The range is organised into three tiers. The Novello Range is aimed at youthful, vibrant and fresh wines, its name taken from the Italian word for "new".
