Yeringberg sits in the central Yarra Valley, on land Baron Frederic-Guillaume de Pury bought in 1863 after migrating from Switzerland in 1852. He purchased 450 hectares and planted 32 hectares of vineyard the same year, running sheep and cattle alongside the vines from the outset. The producer's own account treats this founding date as the start of continuous operation on the site, and the record gives no later change of ownership.

The founder married Adelaide (Ada) Augusta Ibbotson, and the couple had two sons, George (born 1870) and Victor (born 1873). George became heir at 20, after his father died on a visit to Switzerland, and later passed on his knowledge of the property's pre-1921 viticulture and winemaking to his own son, Guillaume. Guillaume and his wife Katherine had four children, born between 1959 and 1964, of whom two now run the operation: David de Pury returned in 1997 as viticulturist and farm manager after research work overseas, and Sandra de Pury returned in 2000 as winemaker after working as a chef and management consultant. The two gradually took on the day-to-day running of the farm, vineyard and winery.

The record names one variety, Marsanne, historically labelled White Hermitage. A bottle of the 1914 vintage was delivered to HMS Renown for the Prince of Wales during his 1920 royal tour of Australia, and the producer states its wines have won awards in Australia, London, Bordeaux, Paris, Brussels and San Francisco, including a gold medal at the 1915 Panama-Pacific International Exposition. No other varieties, vintages or current wine styles are stated on the source page, and no information on winemaking practices, organic or biodynamic status, pricing, or cellar door hours is given there either. The Harvester's confidence notes describe the page as a family history rather than a visitor-facing information page, which explains the gap.

The de Pury family describes its relationship to the land in terms of stewardship rather than title.

The same page adds that each generation realises the positive impact it can have on the future of the land it loves. Read alongside the sequence of return dates for David and Sandra, and the passing of knowledge from George to Guillaume, this reads as a description of a working succession rather than a slogan. The property held a 150-year retrospective tasting in the original winery, featuring wines back to the 1976 vintage, which is the only marker in the record of how far back the current cellar's holdings run.
