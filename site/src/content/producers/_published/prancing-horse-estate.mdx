Prancing Horse Estate is an estate winery at Red Hill South, in the Red Hill subregion of the Mornington Peninsula in Victoria. Two blocks were first planted on the property in 1985, set on a moderate slope divided between a northeast-facing aspect and a west-facing one. The property is surrounded by bush and pasture and overlooks Western Port Bay and Bass Strait.

The producer states that it farms organically and biodynamically, and that the aim is to bring all of its wines to biodynamic standard. No certifying body is named on the page. That is not unusual among growers who describe themselves as biodynamic: certification is a formal process with its own cost and timeline, and some producers apply the practices without seeking the paperwork that would let them call the wine certified. The record here gives no certifier, so none is claimed for this entry either.

Guidance on biodynamic winemaking comes, according to the producer's own account, from partnerships with Pascal Marchand in Burgundy and Patrick Piuze in Chablis.

Vineyard work draws on animals kept on the property. Three horses, named Licorice, Duchess and Molly, are used in cultivation and vineyard management. A flock of sheep continues that work and feeds into the property's composting approach, which the producer ties directly to its organic and biodynamic practice.

A sculpture titled "Wild One", by the artist Deborah Halpern, stands within the vineyard itself. The producer notes that the same work appears on every bottle the winery produces, which makes the sculpture part of the estate's visual identity as well as a fixture of the vineyard.

All fruit is sourced from the estate's own blocks rather than bought in, according to the record. No varieties are named on the page this record was drawn from, so none are listed here.
