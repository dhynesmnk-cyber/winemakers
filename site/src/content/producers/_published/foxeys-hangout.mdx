Foxeys Hangout is an estate winery at Red Hill, in the Mornington Peninsula, with a cellar door and kitchen set above the vineyard. The vineyard itself was planted by Tony and Michael Lee together on the Red Hill property, and the fruit for the wines comes from that estate.

Before the winery, the two brothers ran the Argo Hotel in South Yarra, a venue specialising in Victorian and fine European wines, from the early 1990s. The hotel was sold in 2002, which freed Tony to move with his family to the Red Hill property that same year and turn his attention to Foxeys Hangout full time. The producer states that the winery's first vintages were harvested sometime in the two decades since, though no specific founding year is given for the winery itself.

The producer describes three key vineyards as managed biodynamically and states that the winery holds full organic certification. Neither the organic certifier nor a biodynamic certifying body is named on the page, so the record is entered here as organic certified and biodynamic practising rather than biodynamic certified. The stated approach is one of minimal intervention in the vineyard, summarised in Tony Lee's own words:

<Pull attribution="Tony Lee, Foxeys Hangout About Us page">
  The more we get right in the vineyard, the less we do in the winery.
</Pull>

Winemaking responsibilities are split. Michael Lee is responsible for the sparkling wine, made in the traditional method and disgorged as part of a programme run through the cellar door, with the producer's page citing Champagne as the benchmark he works towards. Michael's background is in hospitality rather than viticulture: before Foxeys Hangout he co-operated Melbourne cafes and restaurants with Tony, including Les Halles and Garçon, alongside their mother Margaret. Chris Strickland holds the head winemaker role. He joined the operation during his university years as a cellar door all-rounder and vineyard helper, switched from a science degree to a Bachelor of Winemaking, and has since worked a vintage in the Old World, taken part in Australia's pinot noir workshop, and sat as an associate wine show judge. Tony Lee's own training includes an economics degree, a chef's qualification, and winemaking study at Charles Sturt University in the 1990s, after work experience in the Yarra Valley during his school years.

The named varieties are pinot noir and a Champagne-style sparkling wine, with the specific sparkling blend components not stated on the page. No pricing, opening hours, or production volume are published in the record, so those fields are left unset here rather than estimated.
