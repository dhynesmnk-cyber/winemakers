Kerri Greens Wine is a Mornington Peninsula label founded by Lucas and Alyce Blanck, a couple who manage their own vineyard sites at Balnarring and Red Hill rather than working from a single leased block. The producer also leases and manages other vineyards around the Peninsula, extending its estate holdings beyond the two named sites. There is no cellar door; wines are sold at local markets and through pop-up events held through the year, and customers can also join a running wine club, The Creatures Club, which lets them set the quantity and frequency of their own deliveries. A ten per cent discount is offered on a first order to anyone who joins the mailing list.

Lucas Blanck's winemaking background traces to Domaine Paul Blanck in Alsace, a family connection the producer states directly. That heritage surfaces in a winemaking choice noted in the record: old oak is used in the Riesling, a technique more associated with Alsace than with the crisper, stainless-driven Rieslings more common on the Peninsula. The current release list named in the record includes a Riesling (Citrea), a Rosé (Rose de Mornington), and a Pinot Noir (Foothills), alongside a fourth wine, Hickson, whose variety the record does not make clear.

The producer states that its fruit is farmed organically, though no certifying body is named for that claim, which is recorded here as practising rather than certified. That is an ordinary position for a grower operating across several small, self-managed and leased sites on the Peninsula: certification is a formal and costly process, and a producer selling through markets and a direct wine club rather than a certified export channel carries less commercial pressure to hold the paperwork. The absence of a named certifier is reported here as a fact about the record, not as a judgement on the viticulture itself.

Because Kerri Greens has no cellar door, the standard route to the wines is the mailing list and the pop-up calendar, along with the direct wine club for anyone content to receive scheduled deliveries. Buyers wanting a specific bottle rather than a subscription arrangement would need to track the pop-up schedule directly, since the record lists no retail stockists beyond the producer's own channels.
