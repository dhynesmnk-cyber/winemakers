Gemtree works in McLaren Vale, in South Australia, on vines the producer describes as decades old. That description, together with the site's account of working its own patch of ground, is the basis for recording the operation here as an estate winery rather than as a buyer of fruit. No street address, suburb or subregion is published on the site, so the location in this entry sits at the level of the region and no finer.

<Pull attribution="Gemtree, on its website">
  Gifted the opportunity to work a unique patch of the earth in South Australia's McLaren Vale.
</Pull>

The farming claims are the substance of what the site publishes. The producer describes organic and biodynamic philosophies in the vineyard, and names no certifying body for either. Under this directory's rules that puts both at practising rather than certified. The distinction matters to readers who are looking for a certificate: organic and biodynamic certification in Australia is administered by a small number of bodies, takes several years of audited conversion, and carries an ongoing cost, so a grower can farm to the standard for a long time before anyone is entitled to print a logo. What can be reported here is that the producer states the practice. Nobody at this directory has inspected the vineyard, and no certificate has been sighted.

Vine age is the one piece of vineyard detail the site quantifies, and it quantifies it loosely. Decades-old is a description, not a planting year, and no founding year for the business appears anywhere on the page. That gap is left open here rather than filled with an estimate.

The Gemtree Tasting Room is currently closed. The producer states this plainly, without publishing a reopening date, an appointment line or an interim arrangement, so a reader planning a McLaren Vale run should treat this address as unavailable rather than as one to ring ahead about. The cellar door status here will change only when the producer publishes something that says it has.

Direct sales are handled through a wine club. The producer states that members can receive up to 20% off wine purchases and get access to offers through the year, which is the standard structure for a mailing list at this end of the market: the discount is the recruiting device, and the offers do the work of moving allocations without a retailer in between. No membership fee, no minimum commitment and no shipping terms are published, so none are recorded.

Beyond that, the source page is thin. It names no varieties, no vineyard blocks, no winemaker, no vessels and no production figure, and it states no prices for individual wines. Those fields are left empty here rather than guessed at from what a McLaren Vale estate of this description would usually be doing.
