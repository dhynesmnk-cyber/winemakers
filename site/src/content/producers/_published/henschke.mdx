Henschke is based in Keyneton, in South Australia, where the family has grown grapes and made wine since the 1860s. Johann Christian Henschke, a stonemason and wheelwright by trade, immigrated from Kutschlau in Silesia in 1841 and first settled in the Adelaide Hills before buying land in the Barossa Valley. He planted a small vineyard in the Eden Valley in the early 1860s, and the first recorded sale of Henschke wine was in 1868, the year the record gives as the winery's founding. Early vintages from that vineyard produced around 300 gallons a year; by 1873 output had grown to 1,600 gallons.

The property passed through the same family, transferred, according to the producer, from father to son. Paul Gotthard Henschke took over the Keyneton property in 1872, for a nominal five shillings, and in 1891 he purchased land near the Gnadenberg Church that became the Hill of Grace vineyard. The original vines there were planted around 1860 by Nicolaus Stanitzki, whose granddaughter later married into the Henschke family. Paul Alfred Henschke, known as Alf, extended the original stone cellar with brick and cement fermentation vats and underground tanks. His son Cyril took over the winery in 1950 and by the mid-1950s was recognised as a pioneer of single-varietal and single-vineyard winemaking in Australia. Cyril later undertook a study tour of winemaking regions in Germany, California and South Africa on a Churchill Fellowship, the first awarded to an Australian winemaker, and was a founding member of the Barons of the Barossa in 1975.

The record names two single-vineyard wines from Eden Valley, Mount Edelstone and Hill of Grace, both shiraz, alongside a historical mention of a "dry red and dry white hock" made in the early years of the business. Hill of Grace draws on old vines planted around 1860, among the oldest continuously producing shiraz plantings recorded anywhere in the country.

The Harvester's record gives no detail on current cellar door hours, tasting arrangements, or pricing, so none is set out here. Fruit source is recorded as estate, drawn from vineyards the family has held across Eden Valley, Barossa Valley and Adelaide Hills, though the source page does not state explicitly whether every wine in the current range is estate-grown or whether any parcels are bought in. Henschke does not report organic or biodynamic certification in this record, and no winemaking practice such as wild fermentation, or wines left unfined, unfiltered, or made with minimal sulphur, is stated as current practice. What the page documents instead is the family's own account of its history: six generations of the same surname, the same handful of properties, and vines still in the ground from the nineteenth century.
