Sedgley & Sons operates from Creadon Farm at 181 Coolart Road, Hastings, on the Mornington Peninsula. The record gives no founding year, so nothing here is claimed about when the vineyard was planted or the business began, only what is stated about the vines themselves.

The estate runs to one hectare of Pinot Noir at Creadon Farm, described in the record as high-quality, non-irrigated fruit. The Harvester's notes flag a contradiction worth repeating rather than resolving: one part of the source states the vines are 25 years old, another states 21 years old. Both figures are preserved here because neither can be confirmed against the other, and no planting year is given that would settle it.

The wine itself is a single variety from a single named site: Pinot Noir off the Creadon Farm block, matured in a combination of new and old French oak barrels for eleven to twelve months before bottling. The record names no other fruit source, so the estate is the only one referenced, and no other variety appears anywhere in the material supplied.

Winemaking is credited to Rick McIntyre, named in the record as being of Moorooduc Estate. What relationship that represents, whether he is employed by Sedgley & Sons, contracted for the vintage, or consulting more loosely, is not stated, and the entry does not guess.

Contact runs through Michael Sedgley directly, by phone, which is the arrangement the record documents for both sales and tasting. The property is described as open most weekends, but tasting and purchase are specifically arranged by calling ahead, so a visit here is booked rather than dropped in on. Pricing is not published: the record states that prices are available on request, scaled to order size and quantity, and that free delivery is offered to the Mornington Peninsula, and separately to Melbourne and the Mornington Peninsula, for orders of a dozen bottles or more. No dollar figures accompany either statement.

Beyond wine, the record notes that Creadon Farm also produces quince paste, from Smyrna and Champion quinces grown on the property, and lemons. These sit alongside the Pinot Noir as farm produce rather than as separate wine styles, and nothing in the record suggests they are sold under the Sedgley & Sons label as wine products.

The wine is stocked beyond the farm gate at a number of named retailers and venues, including Lancemore Lindenderry, Cellarbrations, Sanctuary Inn, The Heritage, Armadale Cellars, Bottle O Hastings, Hotel Lona, Lona St Kilda, Devil Bend Farm and Donovans St Kilda. No online shop is referenced in the record, and no claim of national shipping is made, so purchase is understood here as either direct through Michael Sedgley or through one of these listed outlets.

The record supports no organic or biodynamic determination, no certification, and no production volume, and none of these appear in the entry as a result. What can be said plainly is narrower: a one-hectare estate at a named farm on the Mornington Peninsula, growing Pinot Noir under non-irrigated conditions, made under an outside winemaker's hand, and sold by appointment and through a short list of named stockists.
