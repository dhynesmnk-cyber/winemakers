Murdoch Hill is an estate winery at Oakbank, in the Adelaide Hills, with vineyards in the cooler Lenswood subregion. The Downer family has farmed around Oakbank since 1939. The family's Oakbank property was first planted to vines in 1998 by parents Julie and Charlie Downer, and 2023 marked the 25th year of winemaking from that planting. Brothers Michael and Andrew Downer now produce the Murdoch Hill range.

The two estate vineyards are managed without herbicides or pesticides, using only organic fungicides. The producer does not name a certifying body for this, so the practice is recorded as organic farming without certification, which is the ordinary arrangement for growers at this scale: certification is a separate cost and process that a small estate does not always take on even when the farming itself meets the standard. Wild yeasts are used in fermentation, and the producer describes gentle extraction without additions or manipulation.

The estate vineyards grow Syrah and Chardonnay. Sauvignon Blanc also appears in the range, but the 2023 vintage, the first under that label, was made from sourced grapes under winemaker Brian Light rather than from the home blocks. The Apollo, the estate's flagship, is recorded as 100% Lenswood fruit in both its 2022 and 2023 releases. The Rocket Chardonnay undergoes full malolactic fermentation; the 2023 vintage used 50% new oak across French and Hungarian barrels in both small and large format, and the 2024 vintage is described as coming from the elevated slopes of Lenswood. The Landau Syrah is also estate-grown.

Recorded pricing for current releases runs from $38 for the 2025 Murdoch Hill Chardonnay to $93 for the 2024 Rocket Chardonnay, with the 2023 Landau Syrah at $56. A number of earlier vintages, including the 2022 and 2023 Apollo and the 2022 Orion Syrah, are listed as sold out.

The Oakbank Tasting Room is open 11am to 4pm, Thursday to Tuesday, and closed Wednesday. The producer describes the setting as a gum-dotted family farm, with seating suited to winter and a sun-filled deck in the warmer months. A portion of each bottle sold is donated, according to the producer, to Oz Harvest for food rescue and food relief services.
