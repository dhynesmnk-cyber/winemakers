Verdun Park Wines is an estate winery at Verdun, in the Adelaide Hills, South Australia. The property was purchased in 1993, and the first Shiraz vines were planted there in 1997, the year the record gives as the winery's founding. The producer describes itself as a family business, created by Danielle Voumard with her parents Sandy and Bob Voumard.

Danielle Voumard is General Manager, with more than twenty years in the wine industry and previous roles at Shaw and Smith, Nepenthe, Petaluma, Tatachilla, Lion Nathan, Pernod Ricard and Wine Australia. Bob Voumard, a director, spent five decades in finance, with an accounting background in agriculture and aquaculture, and grew up in Port Lincoln. Sandy Voumard, also a director, had a 37-year career as a primary school teacher before retiring in 2008.

The estate grows Shiraz and Sauvignon Blanc, with fruit drawn from the Verdun vineyard rather than bought in. The winemaking notes describe small parcels of cool-climate wine, made under what the producer calls sustainable viticulture, boutique winemaking and limited transport.

From 2020 the wines have been packaged using dry goods, including glass, manufactured in South Australia. No certifier is named for organic or biodynamic status, and the page makes no claim to either: the sustainability statement stands on its own, without an organic or biodynamic label attached to it.

The first wine was released in 2009 and won a gold medal at the Adelaide Hills Wine Show that same year. That is the only award the record states, and no later results are given.

The winery previously ran a cellar door and hosted events including Crush and Winter Reds. The record is explicit that this has changed: Verdun Park Wines now operates as an online business, with wine also available at select stores, rather than through a cellar door. Orders are sent by Australia Post, Australia wide. No pricing, opening hours or cellar door details appear on the source page, so none are given here.

The record's own dates sit slightly out of order: the property is said to have been purchased in 1993, four years before the first vines went in during 1997. Both dates are stated on the producer's page, and this entry carries them as given rather than resolving the gap.
