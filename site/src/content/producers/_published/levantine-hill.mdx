Levantine Hill is a single estate in the Yarra Valley, rising from the Yarra River at 75 metres to a peak of 225 metres. The record describes the original site as a hill of steep slopes and rocky outcrops, ground the estate's own history states was considered impossible to plant by experts, and one that required a diamond-tipped drilling rig to clear before vines could go in.

<Pull attribution="Levantine Hill, on their estate page">

</Pull>

The estate names two single vineyard sites, Maroondah Highway and Hill Road, both drawing on steep clay loam slopes that the producer compares to the elevations of the Grand Cru vineyards of Chablis. The record does not state a founding year for the company itself; it notes only that the original vineyard has been producing since the 1990s, which is a fact about the fruit rather than about when Levantine Hill was established as a business. No address, suburb or founder is given, and no ownership statement names a family, so the description of a family vineyard is reported here as a claim the estate makes about itself rather than a confirmed structure.

All fruit is grown on the estate, across blocks the record calls ultra-low yielding. Six styles are named: a Sauvignon Blanc Semillon blend, a Rosé of Cabernet Sauvignon, straight Pinot Noir, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah. No wild ferment, unfined, unfiltered or minimal-SO2 practices are stated, and none of the four is claimed here. The winemaking note in the record is limited to a single line, that the estate bottles its premium wines only in what it calls impeccable years, which reads as a statement about vintage selection rather than a technical detail the directory can otherwise verify.

The property is described as varied rather than flat: slopes, amphitheatres, valleys and multiple paddocks are said to create distinct microclimates across the site. Set apart from the vineyard blocks is a truffière, planted in the coolest part of the property on well-drained soil and producing French black truffles alongside the family blocks that surround the Homestead. This is the only secondary product the record names, and it is treated here as a fact about land use on the estate rather than as an invitation to visit.

No hours, tasting fees, pricing or booking information appear anywhere in the record, so cellar door status is recorded as none rather than assumed. No online shop is mentioned. No production volume is stated, so the entry records the band as unknown rather than estimating one. No people are named in connection with the estate, and no certification is claimed for organic or biodynamic practice, so both are recorded as none. What the record supports is a description of a single Yarra Valley site, its two named vineyard blocks, the varieties grown on them, and the truffière that sits alongside the vines.
