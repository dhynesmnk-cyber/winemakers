Miceli sits in the Main Ridge Wine Area on the southern Mornington Peninsula, planted at 250 metres above sea level on north-west-facing slopes with the sea on three sides. Anthony and Pauline Miceli established the vineyard and winery in 1991, after searching for a cool site with the slopes, aspect and soils suited to Burgundian varieties. Pinot Grigio was added in 1997.

The estate runs to roughly 2.5 acres each of Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Pinot Grigio, planted in bands so each variety crosses both soil types on the property: a red, deep Kraznozem clay of volcanic origin, and a granitic-derived sandy clay with grey topsoil and orange subsoil elsewhere on the block. Rows run north-south and are terraced against the slope to maximise sun exposure and minimise shading. The site is described as virtually frost-free and shielded from fog by the surrounding topography, though it is exposed to regular wind, met with adapted trellising and planted windbreaks.

Pinot Noir is planted to the MV6, D2V5 and D5V12 clones, all on own roots since 1991. Chardonnay carries the P56, D10V1 and D10V5 clones, also on own roots from the same planting year. The Pinot Grigio derives from material originally sourced through Kathleen Quealy's plantings, put in the ground in 1997 on Schwartzman rootstock, chosen partly as insurance against phylloxera and for the shorter growing season a shallower rooting depth allows.

Picking is timed to cool mornings, with grapes usually brought in at 10 to 14 degrees ambient temperature, and the winemaking is described on the record as oxidative. Whites are whole-bunch pressed and left to settle naturally before any sulphur addition. Chardonnay is fermented in tank or barrel and spends around a year in contact with yeast lees, while Pinot Grigio is fermented warm, at 24 to 29 degrees, for a savoury rather than overtly fruity style. Pinot Noir follows several parallel paths within a single vintage: some fruit macerates for a week before a wild yeast ferment, some ferments cool and is pressed to dryness, some ferments warmer with part of the batch drained early for barrel fermentation, and some is finished without oak contact, ageing in tank on lees before bottling. A blend of the three estate varieties, made by methode champenoise, is bottled as a sparkling wine under the name Michael.

The premium wines are named after the Micelis' children.
