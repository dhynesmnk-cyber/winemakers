Hurley Vineyard sits at 101 Balnarring Rd, Balnarring, on the Mornington Peninsula in Victoria. The site names Kevin Bell and Tricia Byrnes as the owners; no founding year is given in the record, so none is stated here.

The producer describes itself in vigneron terms specific to a single variety:

<Pull attribution="Hurley Vineyard, on their vineyard page">
  Specialist Pinot Noir vignerons in Balnarring on the Mornington Peninsula in Victoria, Australia
</Pull>

Pinot Noir is the only variety named in the record, and the Harvester's determination treats the fruit source as estate on the strength of that vigneron language and the property's location, while noting that the site does not state explicitly that the fruit is estate-grown. That distinction is worth keeping in mind: the entry reports the determination, not a first-hand inspection of the vineyard.

The current release is the 2024 vintage, listed as four labels: The Estate, Lodestone, Hommage and Garamond. Garamond is recorded as sold out, leaving The Estate, Lodestone and Hommage available for tasting and sale. Earlier vintages are also sold at the cellar door, and wine can be ordered online at cellar door prices.

The cellar door is open every weekend, Saturday and Sunday, from 11am to 5pm, and no booking is needed within those hours. Outside that window, the producer offers appointments by phone or email, contact details given on the site itself rather than reproduced here as a claim of availability.

One detail in the record falls outside the usual run of vineyard facts: Hurley Vineyard states that its wine labels carry information in Braille, and describes this as the first instance of the practice in Australia. That is a producer statement rather than an independently verified claim, but it is specific enough to report as stated.

No organic or biodynamic certification is claimed anywhere in the record, and no practices such as wild ferment, unfined or unfiltered bottling, or minimal sulphur use are mentioned. The record is silent on production volume, so no case figure or band beyond "unknown" can be given, and silent on cellaring vessels, so none are listed.

The overall picture from the record is a small, named-owner Pinot Noir property with a settled weekend cellar door routine and a stated, specific point of difference in its labelling, rather than a business making broader claims about certification, scale or history.
