Wantirna Estate sits in the Yarra Valley, in Victoria's south east not far from central Melbourne. The vineyard was planted in 1963, and the record describes it as the first of the new generation vineyards in the valley, planted well ahead of the district's broader replanting through the 1970s and 1980s. The impetus came earlier still: Reg Egan, a Melbourne lawyer and farmer's son from Tolmie in the state's north east, married Bertina Curcio in 1956, and the couple's interest in wine and food led them to the history of winemaking in Victoria and eventually to the block itself. Reg practised law until 1984, when he gave it up to concentrate on the vineyard full time.

The early plantings included crouchon, pedro ximenez, barbera and dolcetto. In the 1970s the estate consolidated around chardonnay, pinot noir, cabernet sauvignon, cabernet franc, merlot and petit verdot, and much of the cabernet sauvignon and merlot now in the ground dates to the original 1963 vines. That gives the estate old vines by any reasonable measure, since the planting year is on the record rather than asserted loosely.

Winemaking passed from Reg, who did everything himself in the early years, to his daughter Maryann Egan, who holds a degree in wine science from Charles Sturt University and worked for six years at Domaine Chandon making sparkling and table wines before returning to Wantirna Estate after the birth of her first child in 1996. Both Reg and Bertina spent years travelling to Europe to visit vineyards and winemakers, and Maryann worked a vintage in Burgundy during her own time in Europe. Reg still describes himself with a title that is more self-deprecating than technical:

The operation is presented on its own site as a family concern, and the record names the family involved: Reg Egan and Bertina Curcio as founders, Maryann Egan as winemaker, and their wider family taking part in picking, pruning and the rest of the year's vineyard work.

The record makes no organic or biodynamic claim and names no certifier for either, so neither is listed here. There is no published cellar door information, no hours, no tasting fee and no pricing in the record, and none of those is invented to fill the gap. Reg Egan has also published three books, the most recent in 2012, comparing the countryside and rivers of France and Australia, though that sits outside the winemaking proper and is noted here only because the record carries it as part of the family's account of itself.
