d'Arenberg sits at McLaren Vale, south of Adelaide, on land bought by Joseph Rowe Osborn with the proceeds of racehorses he ran under the assumed name J Rowen between 1895 and 1912. The property was called Bundarra. Eight acres of shiraz went in initially and about four acres survived. Joseph's son Frank took the first vintage in 1912, 100 tonnes at £20 per tonne, made as heavy dry red and port for an export market then paying a Commonwealth bounty of four shillings a gallon. The wine went out labelled Bundarra Vineyards by F. E. Osborn & Sons. The name changed to d'Arenberg in 1965, by mutual agreement, after the Bailey family of Glenrowan in Victoria applied to trademark Bundarra. d'Arenberg was the maiden name of Frank's wife Helena, a pianist who trained at the Elder Conservatorium in Adelaide and at Trinity College of Music in London.

The business has stayed in the family. Frank's son Francis d'Arenberg Osborn, known as d'Arry, took it on at sixteen after production stopped during the war years. d'Arry's son Chester graduated from Roseworthy Agricultural College in 1983 with a Bachelor of Applied Science in Oenology and made his first vintage as chief winemaker in 1984, which was also the year of the first d'Arenberg barrel-fermented chardonnay. The red stripe on the label was taken from the crimson-and-white school tie d'Arry wore at Prince Alfred College.

Farming is certified rather than claimed. The producer states that all estate owned and leased vineyards were certified for organic practices by NASAA in December 2015, and for organic and biodynamic practices by the same body in late 2016. The staged dates reflect the ordinary shape of certification, which runs on a conversion period and an audit trail rather than a single declaration. Sheep are agisted in the vineyards and the producer describes natural pest control management. Chester Osborn is reported to have cut yields by eliminating fertilisation and minimising irrigation. In 2016 the oldest bush vine grenache block was harvested with a Clydesdale horse, drays instead of vehicles, to keep soil compaction down; the trial ran several years. The Money Spider Roussanne 2018 is named as the first certified organic wine.

Plantings run wide. Shiraz, cabernet sauvignon, grenache and mourvèdre sit alongside white Rhône varieties Chester planted in 1996: ten acres of marsanne, nine of roussanne and fourteen of viognier. Riesling, chardonnay, semillon, tempranillo, sagrantino, cinsault, pinot noir, graciano and arneis all appear in the wine names the source lists, across dry reds and whites, sparkling, fortified and botrytis sweet wines.

In the winery, foot treading during fermentation and basket pressing were reinstated as working methods, and a nineteenth century hydraulic Coq basket press acquired from Yalumba is used, with the producer stating that all d'Arenberg wines are basket pressed. Stainless steel auto fermenters and tanks were installed in earlier decades. A solar system fitted in 2012 is stated to supply 20 to 30 per cent of manufacturing electricity and to cut emissions by 181 tonnes of CO2 a year.

Three venues operate on the site. d'Arry's Verandah Restaurant occupies the 1880s homestead, renovated and extended in 1997. The Stables Wine Immersion Centre opened on 7 September 2012, backed by a $60,000 Australian Government investment, and offers a Blending Bench, structured tastings and geology displays. The d'Arenberg Cube, five storeys set among mourvèdre vines, opened to the public on 14 December 2017 and carries sixteen rooftop umbrellas on hydraulically operated arms, fifteen black and one red.
