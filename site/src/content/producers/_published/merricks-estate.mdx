Merricks Estate sits on Thompson's Lane in Merricks, on the Mornington Peninsula, on a 55 acre property with ten acres under vine. The vineyard was planted in 1977 by the Kefford family, which makes it one of the first commercial vineyards established on the Peninsula, in an area that had previously been known for farming, horticulture and tourism rather than wine. The record credits winemaking of note to the estate from 1982.

The estate grows Chardonnay, Pinot Noir, Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon and a rosé, and the fruit source given for the property is estate grown throughout. The property is described in the source material as sitting on lush, gentle slopes within sight of Western Port Bay, with a woodland and wetlands track running through the land and a cellar door described as rustic. The setting is described as an unchanged hinterland of the Peninsula, a description that ties the property to the district's farming history rather than to a built tourism landscape.

The producer's own material states an aim toward environmental stewardship:

<Pull attribution="Merricks Estate, on their site">
  Striving to create a sustainable environment for flora and fauna
</Pull>

That statement is not accompanied by any claim of organic or biodynamic certification, and the record supports neither determination. It is worth being explicit about this rather than letting a reader infer a certified practice from an environmental statement alone: the two are different things, and the source keeps them separate.

Merricks Estate has been recognised externally as well as describing itself. The record notes a James Halliday five-star winery rating in 2023 and a Winestate Trophy for Best Wine in Class Pinot Noir in 2017. No other awards or ratings appear in the record, and none beyond these should be assumed.

Cellar door access follows a set, limited calendar rather than standard weekly hours: open 12 to 5pm on the first weekend of every month, daily from 27 to 31 December, and on every weekend in January as well as weekends associated with public holidays. Outside those windows the record gives no indication that visits are possible. No pricing, tasting fee, or online shop is stated anywhere in the source material, so none is claimed here.

Ownership is described as clear in the record: the vineyard's founding and continuing identity are tied to the Kefford family name, and no parent company, shared address, or shared contact domain appears anywhere on the site. The property's scale, at ten acres under vine on a 55 acre holding, is consistent with the kind of single-family operation the record describes.
