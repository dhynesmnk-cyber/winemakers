Quealy farms across four named vineyards on the Mornington Peninsula from a base on Bittern-Dromana Road, Balnarring, where the current owners purchased the property in 2003. The oldest fruit on site, a block called Seventeen Rows, was planted in 1982 by Joan and Stan Paul to Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Riesling on a north-facing slope of shallow loam over clay. The remaining Pinot Noir on the Home Vineyard went in in 1995 and supplies the KKO1 bottling.

Three further vineyards make up the rest of the estate's fruit. Warrawee Road was planted in the mid-1990s by Doctors Campbell and Christine Penfold to three clones of Pinot Noir, 114, 115 and MV6, over red clay washed down from Red Hill. Musk Creek Road, at Main Ridge, was planted in 1994 to Pinot Gris, Pinot Noir, Muscat Rouge à Petits Grains and Chardonnay on a north-north-east slope 180 metres above sea level. Tussie Mussie, a former rose and herb farm also on Bittern-Dromana Road, carries three hectares of Pinot Noir and Pinot Gris over red loam. A fifth block, Halarah, at Baldry's Road, Main Ridge, sits on red ferrosol soils formed from weathered basalt.

All fruit is estate-grown. The property holds organic certification, achieved in 2019, though the certifying body is not named on the producer's page. Viticulture is built around canopy management rather than chemical intervention: hard pruning, early shoot selection and a wide row spacing are used to keep vigour down and let sunlight reach the fruit, a response to what the producer describes as a cool region where cloud cover, rather than heat, limits ripening.

Pinot varieties are treated differently from the Italian whites planted alongside them. Leaf plucking is avoided on Pinot Noir and Pinot Gris to preserve acid and slow ripening, while the more fruitful Friulano, Pinot Grigio, Moscato Giallo, Malvasia Istriana and Ribolla Gialla are given more time through autumn to dry out on the vine before picking.
