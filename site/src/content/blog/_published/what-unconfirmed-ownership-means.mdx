This guide documents <Figure of="published" /> independent Australian
winemakers. Of those, <Figure of="ownership" member="unconfirmed" /> carry a
line on their entry saying that the site has not confirmed who owns the
business. That is close to half the guide, and it is the sort of number that
looks like a failure until you know what it is counting.

## What the notice says, and what it does not

The rule this guide is built on is a rule about ownership, not about size, style
or reputation: a producer is listed only where it has no corporate owner. Any
corporate ownership blocks publication, including a minority stake, and
including membership of a multi-label group.

An entry marked confirmed carries a dated source that states who owns the
business. The site makes its independence claim for that entry and names the
evidence on the page.

An entry marked unconfirmed carries no such source. No document that could be
found says who owns it, so the site says that instead, and makes no
independence claim for the entry at all.

<Pull>
Unconfirmed means the owner is unknown to us. It never means a parent is known
and tolerated.
</Pull>

The difference matters because the two states are often read as a confidence
ranking, and they are not one. A producer whose entry says unconfirmed has not
been graded lower. It has been described accurately.

## Why so many

There are three ways to establish who owns a business, and none of them covers
the field.

A producer's own published statement is the route that carries most of this
guide. It works when a winery publishes a page saying who owns it, and small
wineries overwhelmingly do not. An evidence pass over the entries lacking a
source turned up three candidates and none of them held up.

A trade source has the same defect from the other direction. Regional
association registers list who is a member, which is a fact about membership
rather than about ownership.

A registry lookup is the one people expect to solve it, and on its own it does
not. The Australian Business Register identifies the entity operating a
business; it does not carry shareholder data, so an entry there shows which
company trades under a name without showing that the company has no parent
above it. It is used here to reject a producer on a register hit, and to
corroborate a source that already exists. It cannot clear anyone by itself.
The register that would carry that is ASIC's, and extracting it costs money
this project does not have.

## The rule that does not move

There is one thing the unconfirmed state has never been allowed to do, and it
is worth stating plainly because it is the whole reason the state is safe to
publish at all.

A producer with a documented parent company is not published. Not as
unconfirmed, not with a note, not anywhere. Every entry in this guide carries a
parent company of null, and a producer whose name, domain or Australian
Business Number matches the deny-list register is refused regardless of what
else it can show.

Ignorance about an owner is publishable, and it is published with the ignorance
stated. Knowledge of a corporate owner is not publishable at all. The notice on
half these entries is the first of those two things, said out loud.
